# Is a Friend in Need, a Friend Indeed?

This is a speech I gave at Gavel club of Makerere University as a persuasive speech. I have not dared to change a word just to keep my thoughts as I wrote it pure. May be they will change, who know, but friendship is that one topic that I am always excited to share about or learn about in any conversation. 

Good evening, ladies and gentlemen. Let me start by telling you a story. During my ordinary level, I was involved in a heartbreak, one of a kind. A female friend of mine, academically struggling, was in a relationship with one of the top students. In an effort to impress her boyfriend, she sought my help to improve her grades. Surprisingly, as her grades improved, their relationship deteriorated. The chemistry between them seemed to be contingent upon the academic assistance provided by the boyfriend. 

So I started wondering: is friendship really about showing up only when there's a crisis? I mean, should I keep rooting for bad things to happen just to maintain certain friendships?

That brings me to this famous phrase: "A friend in need is a friend indeed." Sure, it rhymes, but does it capture the essence of friendship? Because let’s be honest, that phrase is only telling half the story. It implies that the best friends are the ones who swoop in during tough times. But what about the friends who are there for the good times too?

Now, before we rush to judgment, let’s think about this. Does being there only when things are tough automatically make someone a better friend? Isn’t the ability to celebrate life’s joys just as important?

Think about it. I want you to make two lists. On the first list, write down the friends who are there when things are falling apart. The one you call without a second thought when you check your student portal and a lecturer offers you a retake. You know, the ones who can handle your emotional meltdowns or lend you 50 bucks because your account mysteriously hit zero. One… two… three. Are you still writing? Four? If you can't make it past five, well, you're either lucky or really in trouble.

Now, let’s make another list. This time, I want you to think of friends you can call when something amazing happens, say getting straight As on your student portal. You know, the people you can tell, “I got a scholarship” without feeling like you’re bragging. The ones who won’t secretly hope you trip on your way up the ladder. One… two… three… this one’s tougher, isn’t it?

Here’s the thing: some friends are great in a crisis, but they’re not so great when life is going well. They’re like emotional paramedics, always rushing in when there’s an emergency, but as soon as you’re on your feet, they disappear, waiting for the next disaster. And while it’s nice to have people who’ll pick you up when you’re down, what about the ones who’ll cheer you on when you’re flying high?

Let’s go back to the love birds. Sure, the boyfriend was great during the hard times, but I began to notice a pattern. When grades started improving, the boyfriend retreated, like he was allergic to the lady's success. It got me thinking, what’s the value of a friend who’s only around when you’re struggling? Do they stick around because it makes them feel needed? Does my success make them uncomfortable? Is it only fun to be friends when I’m down on my luck?

And if I’m being honest, I don’t just want a shoulder to cry on. I want a trampoline to jump on! I want friends who’ll celebrate my wins just as loudly as they console my losses. Friends who genuinely rejoice in my achievements, even when they precede their own. Such a friend is capable of opening more doors to see me win. 

Think of it like this: a real friend is like a die-hard Manchester United fan who’s happy for you when your team, Arsenal, wins the Premier League. (And for those of you who aren’t into football, trust me, that’s like a miracle happening.)

So, which type of friend do you want to be? The one who’s always there for the bad times, or the one who’s also there to pop the champagne when things go right? Let’s not reserve friendship for crises. Let’s make it about celebrating each other’s victories, big or small.

The psychology behind this phenomenon is complex. Being there for a friend in need can provide a sense of purpose and fulfilment while celebrating their successes can sometimes lead to feelings of envy or inadequacy. However, true friendship is built on mutual support and joy, regardless of personal circumstances.

In conclusion, let's revisit the phrase, "A friend in need is a friend indeed." Is this a comprehensive measure of true friendship? I believe we all need the emotional maturity to stomach the achievements of others and celebrate them without any hard feelings. That's when we will genuinely wish others well. That's the other side we often overlook when searching for true friends.

Thank you, and you’ve been an incredible audience!
